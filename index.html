How Color Mixing Works

🎨 The Core Building Blocks: Primary & Secondary

Every color in art and nature can be made from three Primary Colors that cannot be created by mixing other colors: Red, Yellow, and Blue. Mixing any two creates a Secondary Color:

+ ➜
Red + Yellow = Orange
+ ➜
Blue + Yellow = Green
+ ➜
Red + Blue = Purple

⚡ How the Spinning Disc Blends Colors

When the dynamo spins the disc at high speed (600 to 3,600 RPM), your eyes cannot separate the two halves in time. A biological phenomenon called Persistence of Vision causes the light signals on your retina to blend together, perceiving a single unified color.

🔬 Physical Paint vs. Digital Screens

🎨 Subtractive (Paint & Ink)

This simulator models physical subtractive color physics (CMY). Physical paints and inks absorb (subtract) light. The more pigments you mix, the deeper and richer the result becomes.

💡 Additive (Screens & Light)

Monitors and phones use additive light (RGB). Colored light beams add together into your eyes, increasing brightness until Red + Green + Blue light creates pure white.

💡 Designer Tips for Great Combos

  • Complementary Pairs: Colors opposite each other on the color wheel (like Blue and Orange, or Red and Green) create vibrant high-contrast pairs side-by-side, and mix into balanced earth tones.
  • Analogous Pairs: Colors next to each other on the wheel (like Cyan and Blue, or Yellow and Orange) produce smooth, soothing, harmonious transitions.
